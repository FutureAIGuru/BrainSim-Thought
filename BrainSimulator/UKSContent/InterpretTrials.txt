MODULEINTERPRET - TRIAL SENTENCES

Open ModuleInterpret, enter one sentence, and press Enter.
The current development interface interprets AND executes the resulting directives.
Inspect Templates, Word attachments, Meanings, and Executed relationships / Query results.
Capitalization and a final period or question mark are optional.

Start with a fresh UKS for a predictable demonstration. BasicWords already supplies
fido is-a dog, dog/cat is-a animal, fido is brown/small, and some body-part facts.
Some examples therefore reinforce existing relationships rather than create them.
Assertions use .! in Meanings, while stored relationships have that directive removed.

1. CLASSIFICATION AND ATTRIBUTES

Sentence                              Expected stored relationship
Fido is a dog.                        [fido->is-a->dog]
Fido is an animal.                    [fido->is-a->animal]
Dogs are animals.                     [dog->is-a->animal]
Cats are animals.                     [cat->is-a->animal]
Fido is brown.                        [fido->is->brown]
Fido is green.                        [fido->is->green]
Dogs are black.                       [dog->is->black]
Cats are large.                       [cat->is->large]

Notice that the noun and adjective target classes select different meanings for
the same word "are". Plural forms are stored words linked from their singular base word
([w:dog->hasPlural->w:dogs], and for verb agreement [w:is->hasPlural->w:are]). A form has
no meaning of its own: the sentence holds the base word marked plural (shown in Word
attachments as "w:dog (plural)"), and the plural does not change the meaning.

2. NOUN PHRASES, VERBS, AND POSSESSION

Sentence                              Expected stored relationship(s)
The black dog is brown.               [dog0->is->black], [dog0->is->brown]
A small cat is an animal.             [cat0->is->small], [cat0->is-a->animal]
Rover is a small dog.                 [rover->is-a->dog], [rover->is->small]
Rover is a big dog.                   [rover->is-a->dog], [rover->is->big]
Dogs are small animals.               [dog->is-a->animal], [dog->is->small]
Fido and Rover are dogs.              [fido->is-a->dog], [rover->is-a->dog]
Fido and Rover are brown.             [fido->is->brown], [rover->is->brown]
The dog and the cat are black.        [dog->is->black], [cat->is->black]
Dogs have tails.                      [dog->has->tail]
Fido has fur.                         [fido->has->fur]
Fido chases cats.                     [fido->chases->cat]
Dogs chase animals.                   [dog->chases->animal]
The dog is chasing the cat.           [dog->chases.ongoing->cat]

A described noun phrase refers to something; it says nothing about its class. "The black dog"
is an existing dog that is black, named or not (on a fresh UKS none is, so dog0 is introduced;
"The brown dog" would be Fido, whom BasicWords makes brown). If several fit, the reply asks
which. "A small cat" and "small cats" name a kind: an anonymous subclass of cat (cat0,
isAnonymousClass), found or made. A description the whole class has tells nothing apart, so
after "Dogs are black." "the black dog" is just the dog. Joining a described kind is joining
its class with its description: in "Rover is a small dog." Rover is a dog and is small, and no
subclass is made (an agent such as ModuleClassCreate may later gather members with attributes
in common into one).
The chase/chases spellings share a meaning. This is vocabulary knowledge, not a
general verb conjugation library. Ongoing actions retain the ongoing modifier.

3. NUMERIC QUANTITIES

Sentence                              Expected stored relationship
Dogs have 4 legs.                     [dog->has.4->leg]
Fido has 3 legs.                      [fido->has.3->leg]
Cats have 2 tails.                    [cat->has.2->tail]

w:0 through w:9 inherit numberWord, which inherits adjective. Their meanings are
the existing numeric Thoughts. Explicit quantity templates currently cover these
ten digits and produce has.N.! directives. This is not yet general numeric
composition. Words such as "three" and multi-digit quantities are not included.

4. NEGATION

Sentence                              Expected stored relationship
Fido is not black.                    [fido->is.not->black]
Cats are not large.                   [cat->is.not->large]
Dogs are not cats.                    [dog->is-a.not->cat]
Fido is not a cat.                    [fido->is-a.not->cat]

These create explicit negative relationships through the existing isNegative
property. Negation is not simply absence of a positive relationship.

5. QUERIES

Try these on the initial knowledge, before the preceding edits change it.

Sentence                              Expected Query results
Is Fido brown?                        [fido->is->brown]
Is Fido a dog?                        [fido->is-a->dog]
Are cats animals?                     [cat->is-a->animal]
What is brown?                        Includes [fido->is->brown]
Is Fido black?                        No matching relationships.
What is Fido?                         [fido->is-a->dog]
What is a dog?                        [dog->is-a->animal]
What does Fido have?                  Includes [fido->has->tail], [fido->has.4->leg]
Do dogs have tails?                   [dog->has->tail]
Do cats have fur?                     [cat->has->fur]
Is Fido not black?                    No matching relationships.
Are dogs not cats?                    No matching relationships.

Query meanings use is.? / is-a.? and the existing UKS query action mechanism.
Execute returns its matching relationships. A yes/no query returns a matching
relationship rather than a separate Boolean. No match is not proof of falsity.
The wildcard query may return more results after additional assertions.

"What does Fido have?" follows inheritance: Fido's tail and legs are inherited
from dog. A yes/no query without a wildcard finds only a direct relationship, so
"Does Fido have a tail?" currently reports no match. The negative queries match
only an explicit negative relationship: enter "Fido is not black." (or "Dogs are
not cats.") first, and the query then returns it.

6. LEARNING AN UNKNOWN NOUN

With a fresh vocabulary, enter:
Pigs are animals.                     Learns w:pigs is-a noun and w:pigs means pigs,
                                      then asserts [pigs->is-a->animal].
Pigs are brown.                       Asserts [pigs->is->brown].

No spelling rule relates an unknown plural to its singular yet (the English-only
singularizer was removed), so "pigs" is learned as a word with its own concept.

The UI permits remembering unknown words. Their grammatical class is learned
directly from the longest winning template. New interpreter words inherit
unknownWordType (under wordType) until classified. A provisional sequence match
can fill a word-class wildcard without making the word itself a wildcard.

The hungry dog                       Learns w:hungry is-a adjective and means hungry,
                                     then introduces a hungry dog: [dog0->is->hungry].

The hungry dog ate dinner             Hungry becomes an adjective.
The hungry should be fed              Not understood; hungry is not learned.

The longer matching phrase wins over its shorter fragments. A sentence that is not
understood (no template covers it from its first word to its last) teaches nothing:
each input is one UKS transaction, undone if it is not understood. So "The hungry
should be fed" (there is no passive template), "Teh dog is brown." and "Rover is a
big dgo." leave the UKS unchanged. A question leaves it unchanged too.
Classification replaces the temporary unknownWordType parent. Known words
inherit word through their grammatical class, without a direct word parent.
"The globular cluster" now matches article-adjective-noun and can classify both
unknown words. This is a syntactic inference, not proof that it is a fixed phrase.

A new word is never learned into a closed class (article, auxiliary, negation and
question words; closedClass in BasicWords.txt). An unknown word matched only on its
own, such as "big" in "Rover is a big dog.", waits while an earlier template could
still cover it; there it becomes an adjective of Rover rather than a bare noun.

UNDO: the Undo button (ModuleInterpret.UndoLastInput) undoes the most recent statement
that changed the UKS: what it stated, the words it taught, and any location history it
recorded. Earlier statements can be undone in turn. The UKS records the changes
(UKS.Journal.cs); weights and firing are not restored.

7. ANSWERS

Each query result is turned back into English by running an assertion template in
reverse (GenerateSentence). The reply appears in the Answer box; Details shows each
stage. Each noun the question used is said as it was there: with the same article (or
none) and the same number. Other nouns are singular where they can be, with "a"/"an" if
they take an article: count nouns do, mass nouns (fur, dinner) and names (Fido) do not, and
a newly learned noun does not. A verb agrees with the subject ("a dog has", "dogs have").
Every answer must satisfy the word order constraints in BasicWords.txt: "a"/"an" before a
consonant/vowel and only before a singular count noun, "the" before a common noun, a number
before a noun, an ongoing verb after a copula, and a plural noun after a digit other than 1.

Sentence                              Expected Answer
Is Fido brown?                        Fido is brown.
Is Fido a dog?                        Fido is a dog.
Are cats animals?                     Cats are animals.
What is Fido?                         Fido is a dog.
What does Fido have?                  Fido has a tail. / Fido has 4 legs.
Do dogs have tails?                   Dogs have tails.
Does a dog have a tail?               A dog has a tail.
Does the dog have a tail?             The dog has a tail.
Do dogs have a tail?                  Dogs have a tail.
Do cats have fur?                     Cats have fur.
Is Fido black?                        I don't know.

No match is not proof of falsity, so an empty result is answered "I don't know."
A statement that is stored is answered "OK."

8. INFLECTED FORMS

Each word form is stored and linked from its base word, which alone has a meaning: singular,
present tense (for a verb, the form used with "it": chases, eats, is, has). The sentence holds
the base word marked with the form's inflection; Word attachments shows it, e.g.
"w:chases (pastTense)". The plural adds no meaning. The past tense adds "past" and the present
participle adds "ongoing" to the verb's meaning. The stored relationship can be said again.

Sentence                              Expected stored relationship / regenerated sentence
Fido chased the cat.                  [fido->chases.past->cat]       Fido chased a cat.
Dogs chased cats.                     [dog->chases.past->cat]        A dog chased a cat.
Fido ate dinner.                      [fido->eats.past->dinner]      Fido ate dinner.
Fido eats dinner.                     [fido->eats->dinner]           Fido eats dinner.
Dogs eat dinner.                      [dog->eats->dinner]            A dog eats dinner.
The dogs are chasing the cats.        [dog->chases.ongoing->cat]     A dog is chasing a cat.
The dog is eating dinner.             [dog->eats.ongoing->dinner]    A dog is eating dinner.
The dog is chases the cat.            Not understood: "is ___" requires the present participle.

The verb phrase "is chasing" is ??.auxiliary ??.actionVerb.presentParticiple: the element
requires the inflection, and its meaning (chases.ongoing.!) is the verb's plus "ongoing".
An element that names no inflection accepts any, so "Fido ate dinner." uses the ordinary
action template, and "The dog chasing the cat." is also accepted, as chases.ongoing.
Irregular forms (ate) are stored exactly like regular ones (chased); there are no spelling
rules yet, so an unstored form is an unknown word.

9. MORE QUESTIONS

On a fresh UKS, first enter these statements:
Dogs chase cats.
Dogs eat dinner.
Fido is in the yard.
The cat is in the kitchen.
Fido was black.
Fido is chasing the cat.

Question                              Expected Answer
What do dogs do?                      Dogs chase a cat. / Dogs eat dinner.
What does Fido do?                    Fido chases a cat. / Fido eats dinner. / Fido is chasing a cat.
Do dogs chase cats?                   Dogs chase cats.
Does Fido chase cats?                 Fido chases cats.
Where is Fido?                        Fido is in a yard.
Where are cats?                       Cats are in a kitchen.
Is Fido in the yard?                  Fido is in the yard.
Is Fido in the house?                 I don't know.
Is Fido brown?                        Fido is brown.
Was Fido black?                       Fido was black.
Is Fido black?                        I don't know.
Was Fido brown?                       I don't know.
Is Fido chasing the cat?              Fido is chasing the cat.
Fido was a dog.                       I don't understand.

"What do dogs do?" asks for every relationship whose type is an action (BasicWords classifies
chases and eats as actions). As in the UKSWeb labs, a location is a place (yard, house,
doghouse, kitchen, inside, outside) and a thing is located at it: "Fido is in the doghouse."
and "Fido is outside." both store [fido->located->...], and "Where is Fido?" asks located.?.
Questions follow inheritance (Fido is a dog) and rules whose conditions are met, as GetAttributes
does, so "Does Fido chase cats?" is answered from "Dogs chase cats." A yes/no question needs the
relationship type it asks about: is.past does not answer "Is Fido black?".
A question template that asksQuestion uses the relationship its own words supply, as a query:
the copula supplies is.! or, in the past tense, is.past.!, so one template asks both "Is Fido
brown?" and "Was Fido brown?". The present and the past are different relationships.
A past-tense word must fill an element whose meaning the template uses: the classification
templates say is-a whatever their copula, so "Fido was a dog." is not understood.

Word attachments shows each word's classes and inflection, e.g. "w:is (copula, pastTense)".

10. PREPOSITIONAL PHRASES AND LOCATION HISTORY

A preposition says where: w:in means located.!, and on, under, near and behind mean dotted
subtypes (located.under.!), so "Where is ...?" (located.?) finds them all. A prepositional
phrase can complete a sentence, describe a noun, or place an event; an event is the source of
its location, as in UKSWeb Lab 4 ([[Fido->plays->fetch]->located->outside]).

Sentence                              Expected stored relationship(s)
The cat is under the table.           [cat->located.under->table]
The ball is near the bed.             [ball->located.near->bed]
Fido is behind the house.             [fido->located.behind->house]
Fido plays fetch outside.             [[fido->plays->fetch]->located->outside]
Fido chased the cat in the yard.      [[fido->chases.past->cat]->located->yard]
The dog in the kitchen is brown.      [dog0->located->kitchen], [dog0->is->brown]
The cat in the kitchen chased the ball.  [cat0->located->kitchen], [cat0->chases.past->ball]

A location is exclusive and records history (BasicWords), so a new location displaces the
old one, which is kept as was.located at the head of the thing's events sequence, newest first.
This is the Lab 4 mechanism, now in the UKS (UKS.History.cs), used by both UKSWeb and the
interpreter. On a fresh UKS, first enter:
Fido is in the yard.
Fido is in the kitchen.
The cat is under the table.
Fido plays fetch outside.

Question                              Expected Answer
Where is Fido?                        Fido is in a kitchen.
Is Fido in the kitchen?               Fido is in the kitchen.
Is Fido in the yard?                  I don't know.
Where is the cat?                     The cat is under a table.
Is the cat on the table?              I don't know.
Where does Fido play fetch?           Fido plays fetch outside.

Fido's events now hold [fido->was.located->yard]. A present-tense question leaves historical
relationships out of its answer.

How a sentence is read: a sentence template describes the whole sentence, so it must start at
the first word and end at the last; phrases can be anywhere. When readings compete, the one
covering the sentence wins: "Fido chased the cat in the yard." places the chase (the event
template covers every word first), while "Is the cat in the kitchen black?" asks about "the cat
in the kitchen" (the locative question would leave "black" over). A question does not state
what its noun phrases describe.

11. TEACHING A NEW KIND OF SENTENCE

When a sentence is not understood, the system asks "Can you say it another way?" The next
input is taken as the answer unless it says otherwise (it is a question, it is not understood
itself, or it shares nothing with the sentence; then it is read as new). On a fresh UKS:

The cat chased Fido and Rover.        I don't understand. Can you say it another way?
The cat chased Fido. The cat chased Rover.
                                      Thanks, I understand now. (I'm still learning ...)

The explanation's meanings are lined up with the sentence by reference: each Thought they
mention (cat, chases.past, fido, rover) is the meaning of a phrase or word of the sentence,
which becomes an element with a new role; "and" is left as a literal word. Details shows the
learned template, as text like the definitions in LoadLanguageKnowledge. Statements made in
one sentence are related, probably happening together, so each chase is conjoinedWith the
other (a commutative relationship). If the explanation leaves something out, the reply says
what ("Your explanation doesn't mention "rover"."), and it can be said again.

A learned template starts weak (confidence 0.25) and each use moves it halfway to certainty;
while below 0.8, the facts it stores take its confidence as their weight and the reply says it
is still learning. The roles constrain only by class, so it already reads
"The dog chased Fido and the cat." Undo forgets the template and everything it stored.
